# Final Reflection

## Final Reflection

Having finished making the first real playable iteration of my game, there is much to reflect upon. My goal was to find out if AI could help a beginner build a game from nothing, all while persevering the developer's creative vision. Having started with no game building experience, I decided to use myself as the test subject. During the last 5 weeks, I got familiar with the GDevelop engine and tirelessly worked on the development of my first game, Deepest Dungeon, a roguelike, dungeon-crawler where the main goal is to go as far down as possible. This was not just me developing a game, but an experiment that focuses on how AI is currently shaping the game industry. In the beginning, I was skeptical. I wasn't sure about AI and whether it was truly practical, but over time those doubts began to fade away. I started my project by researching how AI was currently being used professionally. I learned how both indie developers and large studios are using AI to help streamline the tedious processes of game building such as debugging and prototyping, as well as the negative views that many game developers have in common, especially with ethical and legal concerns. While working alongside the Gdevelop AI Chat and agent, I came across many positives, as well as my fair share of negatives and frustrations. Starting with the positives, I found that the AI was exceptional at teaching you the ropes. When utilizing the agent to help build the more technical parts within the events, it didn't just build the sequences straight away. Instead, it planned out the roadmap and taught me what every component should theoretically do and how it is applied before confirming the process with me and putting it into practice. This was especially helpful in the beginning phases of my game, where most of the mechanics are pretty generic. However, once things got more complicated, this is where the AI started to fail. As more mechanics were added, the bugs began to pile up. I realized that the AI was really only good with the more common issues and followed a more generic path for solutions. The root cause of many major issues were beginning to get even smaller and required more specific solutions; this was the downfall of the AI. The agent's scope was too broad, causing it to occasionally miss the bugs and at times even making things worse. Although the AI was beginning to have a hard time, this was when I realized how much I was actually gaining from the experience. There have been moments where I was the one who had to guide the AI and offer solutions. Looking back at those moments, I realized that was something I never would have thought of before I began this journey. Near the end, I started to find myself building more and more complex event sequences without the AI's assistance, the most notable being the procedural generation mechanic and solving the issue with the random room identifications. In the end, I wasn't just able to build my first game from scratch. I believe I answered more than just my research question of whether AI can help a beginner build a game while preserving their creative voice. I learned that AI is a powerful tool that, when used properly, can even teach you complex tasks, like how to build your own game.
