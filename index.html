Project Identity Card

Current Build Profile

Game Concept Deepest Dungeon

A 2D pixel, top-down, dungeon-descent roguelike bullet-hell game.

Player Goal Go deeper

Clear enemy rooms, find the way down, descend, and survive as long as possible.

AI Role Technical partner

AI helps with planning, event logic, prototyping, and troubleshooting while the creative direction stays mine.

Short Summary

This project explores whether AI can help beginners start developing playable games without advanced coding skills. The focus is accessibility, creative ownership, and the tension between assistance and authorship.

Audience and Context

The audience is beginner game creators, design students, artists, and storytellers who have game ideas but feel blocked by coding or technical barriers.

Playable Prototype

A small dungeon-crawler prototype made with AI support.

Process Journal

A record of where AI helped, where it failed, and where I revised or rejected its ideas.

Beginner Toolkit

A workflow showing how non-coders can use AI while keeping creative control.

Inquiry Brief

What The Research Is Testing

Why It Matters

Game development can feel unapproachable to people who have strong ideas for mechanics, storytelling, or world-building but lack coding skills. AI tools may close that gap by assisting with planning, coding, prototyping, art, and problem-solving.

What I Need To Learn

I need to learn how beginner-friendly AI game-development tools actually are, where AI is most useful in the process, and how to recognize when AI supports creative direction instead of replacing it.

Possible Outcomes

  • AI helps a beginner develop a playable prototype with minimal complications.
  • AI complicates the process or drifts away from the creator's vision.
  • The most useful role for AI is as a technical support system, not a creative replacement.

Depth 08

Lock The Prototype Scope

Goal: Choose the game concept and define the playable prototype scope.

Activities: Review experiment results, decide what kind of small game is realistic, and create a simple outline.

Success indicator: The game idea is small enough to build, but connected to the research question.

Depth 09

Build The First Playable Prototype

Goal: Build the first playable prototype.

Activities: Start the basic game version, use AI for help with coding, logic, debugging, placeholder writing, or planning.

Output: A rough playable prototype and an AI process log.

Success indicator: The prototype can be played, even if it is unfinished or visually simple.

Depth 10

Playtest And Revise

Goal: Playtest, revise, and evaluate creative ownership.

Activities: Ask classmates or friends to test, collect feedback, revise the prototype, and reflect on AI's role.

Output: Revised prototype, feedback notes, and a reflection on AI's role.

Success indicator: I can point to specific examples where my creative decisions shaped the game more than the AI did.

Goal: Prepare the final presentation and process documentation.

Activities: Finalize the documented experiment, organize screenshots/prompts/research notes, and explain what I learned.

Output: Final Command Center, playable prototype or documentation, and final reflection.

Prompt Log

AI Requests That Shaped The Build

Creative planning interview with ChatGPT

Prompt used: I asked ChatGPT to interview me one question at a time about my game loop, mechanics, story, and priorities while keeping the creative vision mine.

Model / tool: ChatGPT

Output summary: It organized my answers into a top-down roguelike dungeon crawler plan with WASD movement, mouse aiming, projectile combat, room clearing, enemies, upgrades, and floor descent.

What changed afterward: I used the plan to decide what to build first in GDevelop.

GDevelop AI support for building logic

Prompt used: Requests focused on technical help inside GDevelop, such as setting up movement, projectile behavior, collisions, and enemy health.

Model / tool: GDevelop AI chat and agent

Output summary: AI helped guide event logic and locate the right actions inside the no-code system.

What changed afterward: I was able to move from tutorials into my own game prototype.

NotebookLM source processing

Prompt used: Summarize source material and help me understand GDevelop, AI tools, and beginner-friendly game development.

Model / tool: NotebookLM

Output summary: It created study artifacts such as summaries, quizzes, and a short overview video.

What changed afterward: I checked the source outputs and used the verified findings to support my research direction.
